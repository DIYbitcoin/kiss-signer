TURN 4 · SIGNING, RUNNING
This one is live and loops, so it can be judged as motion rather than as three stills. One coin is signed at a time, which is what the device actually does: kiss_psbt_sign walks the inputs in order, so the strand going green is a real event and not decoration. The out-side never moves while it runs. Both Sparrow and SeedSigner state the fee as a share of the send once it matters, and KISS already carries the format string for it, so the fee strand now says what slice it is. TURN 3 · 2C, TAKEN FURTHER
The elided middle row is the right answer for a long list, so 3a keeps it and states how many coins are hidden and what they are worth: [ ... ] alone withholds the number that decides whether that strand is the whole transaction. The bundled strand is drawn dashed, so many coins never look like one coin. One thing does not carry over: a 20-coin spend is never a clean screen here, because WPSBT_MERGE_INS is 5, so 3a is drawn with the coins-linked bar sitting directly above an inert HOLD TO SIGN, which is what explains why it is inert. That spend also has no change, so the third strand says so rather than going missing. 3b is what a graph can do that a picture cannot: tap a strand to inspect that coin, with the address and the out-side still on screen.

TURN 2 · THE GRAPH, AND THE MATH
